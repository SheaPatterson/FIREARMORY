Ballistic data describes how a cartridge performs from the moment it leaves the muzzle — covering velocity, energy, and how the bullet behaves downrange. For collectors, this data helps you understand the capabilities of the firearms in your Vault. For shooters, it informs ammunition selection, helps set realistic expectations for range performance, and provides a useful baseline when evaluating different load types side by side. FireArmory Vault surfaces all of this data directly on each caliber's wiki page so you always have it on hand.

<Info>
  Ballistic data in the wiki represents typical published load data. Actual performance varies by firearm, barrel length, and ammunition lot.
</Info>

## Data available for each caliber

Each caliber page in the wiki includes the following ballistic fields:

| Field | Unit(s) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Bullet Weight | Grains (gr) | The weight range of bullets commonly loaded for this caliber |
| Muzzle Velocity | fps / m/s | The speed of the bullet as it exits a standard test barrel |
| Muzzle Energy | ft-lbf / J | The kinetic energy of the bullet at the muzzle, derived from weight and velocity |
| SAAMI Pressure Rating | PSI / MPa | The maximum average pressure specification established by SAAMI for safe operation |
| Trajectory Drop at 100 yd | Inches | Approximate bullet drop at 100 yards relative to a 25-yard zero |
| Trajectory Drop at 200 yd | Inches | Approximate bullet drop at 200 yards relative to a 25-yard zero |

## Understanding the data

Use the explanations below to make sense of the key fields you'll encounter on any caliber's ballistic data page.

**Muzzle velocity** measures how fast a bullet is traveling the instant it exits the barrel, expressed in feet per second (fps) or meters per second (m/s). Higher muzzle velocity generally means a flatter trajectory and more energy delivered downrange, though barrel length, powder charge, and bullet weight all affect the final figure.

**Muzzle energy** expresses the kinetic energy of the bullet at the muzzle in foot-pounds of force (ft-lbf) or joules (J). It is calculated from bullet weight and velocity, so a heavier bullet traveling at moderate speed can produce the same muzzle energy as a lighter bullet traveling much faster. Muzzle energy is a common benchmark when evaluating stopping power or suitability for a particular use case such as hunting or self-defense.

**Trajectory drop** tells you how much the bullet falls below your line of sight at a given distance, assuming a standard zero. Because all bullets begin to drop due to gravity immediately after leaving the muzzle, understanding drop helps you compensate for distance. The wiki expresses drop in inches at 100 and 200 yards relative to a 25-yard zero, giving you a practical reference for common shooting distances.
