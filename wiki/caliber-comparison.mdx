FireArmory Vault's caliber comparison tool lets you place up to four calibers side by side in a single view. You can evaluate their specs, ballistic performance, and common uses without toggling back and forth between individual wiki pages. The comparison tool puts all the data in one place. Use it when you're deciding between two cartridges for a new purchase, learning how a classic pairing stacks up, or building a reference for a specific shooting discipline.

## Using the comparison tool

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Wiki tab">
    Tap **Wiki** in the bottom navigation bar to open the caliber wiki home screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browse or search for the first caliber">
    Scroll through caliber families or use the search bar to find the first caliber you want to compare.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap 'Add to Comparison'">
    On the caliber's wiki page, tap **Add to Comparison**. A comparison tray will appear at the bottom of your screen confirming the caliber has been added.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat for up to 4 calibers">
    Return to the wiki home screen or search results and repeat the previous step for each additional caliber. You can add up to four calibers to a single comparison.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap 'Compare' to view the side-by-side table">
    Tap **Compare** in the comparison tray to open the full side-by-side comparison view. Scroll horizontally to see all selected calibers across every attribute.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you can compare

The side-by-side comparison table includes the following attributes for each selected caliber:

- **Case length** — The length of the cartridge case in inches and millimeters
- **Bullet diameter** — The nominal bullet diameter in inches and millimeters
- **Muzzle velocity range** — The low-to-high velocity range across common load types, in fps
- **Muzzle energy range** — The low-to-high muzzle energy range across common load types, in ft-lbf
- **Common firearm types** — The action types and firearm platforms most frequently chambered for this caliber
- **Typical uses** — The primary applications the caliber is designed or commonly employed for, such as self-defense, hunting, or competition shooting

## Popular comparisons

The caliber comparison tool is especially useful for classic head-to-head matchups. Below are three of the most frequently compared pairings in the Vault community, along with a snapshot of the key differences.

### 9mm Luger vs. .45 ACP

Two of the most iconic handgun calibers — comparing them highlights the classic trade-off between capacity-friendly, lower-recoil performance and large-bore stopping power.

| Attribute | 9mm Luger | .45 ACP |
|---|---|---|
| Bullet Diameter | 0.355 in (9.02 mm) | 0.452 in (11.48 mm) |
| Typical Bullet Weight | 115–147 gr | 185–230 gr |
| Muzzle Velocity Range | 985–1,180 fps | 830–1,050 fps |
| Muzzle Energy Range | 317–410 ft-lbf | 352–452 ft-lbf |
| Common Uses | Self-defense, duty, competition | Self-defense, duty, target shooting |

### .223 Rem vs. .308 Win

A natural pairing for rifle shooters — the lightweight, high-velocity .223 Rem versus the heavier, harder-hitting .308 Win.

| Attribute | .223 Rem | .308 Win |
|---|---|---|
| Bullet Diameter | 0.224 in (5.69 mm) | 0.308 in (7.82 mm) |
| Typical Bullet Weight | 40–77 gr | 110–175 gr |
| Muzzle Velocity Range | 2,750–3,300 fps | 2,500–2,850 fps |
| Muzzle Energy Range | 1,000–1,300 ft-lbf | 2,300–2,750 ft-lbf |
| Common Uses | Varmint, target, defensive rifle | Big game hunting, precision, military |

### .357 Mag vs. .44 Mag

Both iconic revolver cartridges — this comparison illustrates how the .357 Mag favors velocity while the .44 Mag delivers raw energy.

| Attribute | .357 Magnum | .44 Magnum |
|---|---|---|
| Bullet Diameter | 0.357 in (9.07 mm) | 0.429 in (10.90 mm) |
| Typical Bullet Weight | 110–180 gr | 180–300 gr |
| Muzzle Velocity Range | 1,200–1,800 fps | 900–1,500 fps |
| Muzzle Energy Range | 500–825 ft-lbf | 740–1,200 ft-lbf |
| Common Uses | Self-defense, hunting, target | Big game hunting, target shooting |

## Case images

Every caliber page in the wiki includes a case image showing the cartridge's full profile. These images are drawn to scale relative to other calibers. This lets you immediately grasp the physical size difference between, for example, a 9mm Luger and a .45 ACP, or a .223 Rem and a .308 Win. Case images are especially handy when you're visually identifying an unfamiliar cartridge or explaining caliber differences to a new shooter.

When you run a comparison, case images for all selected calibers appear side by side at the top of the comparison view. This gives you a visual anchor before you dig into the numeric data.
